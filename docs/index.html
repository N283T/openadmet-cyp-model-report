OpenADMET CYP Blind Challenge · closed 2026-11-03

CYP inhibition and TDI prediction for 750 blinded compounds

Post-challenge model report by N283T: regression rank 27 of 103 and classification rank 7 of 62 on the final leaderboard.

Regression · final Rank 27 / 103 MA-ST-RAE 0.4432 · Tier 8
Classification · final Rank 7 / 62 MA-MCC 0.4034 · Tier 2

0. TL;DR

  • Two tracks. Regression (direct-inhibition pIC50 for CYP1A2, 2C9, 2D6 and 3A4) finished 27th of 103, mid-table. Classification (time-dependent inhibition calls for CYP2D6 and 3A4) finished 7th of 62, in the second significance tier behind three entries.
  • Every regression column is a per-endpoint ensemble of tabular readouts (TabPFN, TabICL, LightGBM) over descriptors, frozen pretrained embeddings and Boltz-2 trunk features. The output was rescaled to a fixed mean and spread. That single placement step was the largest regression gain of the whole challenge, and it deserves a caveat (section 5).
  • The one idea I would keep from classification: every supplied CYP2D6 TDI label sits on a primary-screen hit, so molecules that were clearly inactive in the primary screen were added as pseudo-negatives. This fixed a coverage defect in the training labels, and the device validated on labels it never saw.
  • The last month taught one lesson: small cross-validation gains did not transfer. Nearly every late regression blend that looked better in development was worse on the leaderboard, and the final entries are simply the best-observed columns.

2. Results

The scored entries are the last uploads, both made on 2026-11-03.

Three boards, one entry

Where a target's predictions did not change after the 09-24 Interim entry (regression 2C9 and 3A4, classification 3A4), the Final value and its bootstrap standard deviation match Interim to every printed digit. The Final board therefore scores the full test set, like Interim. That makes Interim against Final the one clean comparison of what the last six weeks bought. The answer is CYP2D6 only: regression 2D6 ST-RAE went from 0.6104 to 0.5852 and 2D6 TDI MCC from 0.2573 to 0.3046. Nothing else moved.

Final, Interim and Live ranks are not comparable with each other: the boards hold different sets of entries (the Final board lists 103 and 62 entries against 282 and 164 users on the last Live board).

3. Data and building blocks

Most members are built from the same few pieces. Each piece is named here once, and the ensemble tables below use the short names.

Data

  • Challenge data. Direct-inhibition pIC50 for the four isoforms, the TDI labels, the pIC50 measured under the TDI condition (after preincubation with NADPH), and the single-concentration primary screen (log2fc, about 49.5 µM).
  • Public CYP data. ChEMBL 36, PubChem AID 1851 (the Veith P450-Glo screen) and Tox21 P450-Glo, merged into 156,372 observations on 35,320 compounds for the multi-task encoders. A CYP2C9 specialist encoder used 59,255 direct 2C9 observations on 33,281 compounds from ChEMBL, PubChem, Tox21, TDC and Novartis. Every connectivity block shared with the challenge was removed first.
  • No proprietary data. Pretrained models that were used are listed in the next table.

Readouts and combiners

  • Readouts. TabPFN 2.6 (8 estimators), TabPFN 3.5-fast, TabICL v2 (8 estimators), and LightGBM with an L1 objective (one member tuned with Optuna). A TabPFN 3.5 “fast” and “full” run of the same recipe correlate at 0.987–0.997.
  • Equal mean where the evidence did not support fitted weights.
  • Caruana selection: greedy forward selection with replacement, starting empty, adding the member that most lowers the pooled ST-RAE of the running mean. The step count is chosen from {5, 10, 20, 50} by an inner cross-validation. Deployed weights are the mean of the five outer-fold weight vectors, which is why they are fractional.
  • Moment matching: rescale a column to a reference mean and standard deviation, ref.mean + (x − x.mean) / x.std × ref.std, so only the ranking changes. It is applied per fold in development and once to the final blind column. See section 5.

4. The regression ensembles

Each endpoint has its own column, built and selected separately. “OOF” is series-grouped five-fold out-of-fold ST-RAE (lower is better) and is on a different scale from the leaderboard. The standalone numbers are for the member alone.

CYP2C9: five equal members

CYP2D6: three branches and a small blend

  • Core, weight 0.4. The equal mean of jeremy_scores_log2fc_tabpfn26 and jeremy_finetuned_log2fc_lgbm, the two-member subset found by target-wise pruning. OOF 0.828.
  • Robust bound branch, weight 0.4. LightGBM (300 trees, 15 leaves) on the same features with a pseudo-Huber loss (δ = 0.5), plus a one-sided pseudo-Huber penalty above pIC50 4.0 on 2,823 compounds with a weak primary response (log2fc cutoff −0.5), constraint weight 0.35. It is blended 50/50 with the core after per-fold moment matching. Alone it is worse in ST-RAE (1.010) but ranks better; blended it gives 0.820 (parent) and 0.810 (scaffold) against 0.828 and 0.822 for the control.
  • Boltz trunk branch, weight 0.2. pxr_core--trunk_molecular--lgbm_l1: Boltz-2 trunk features with CheMeleon and Mordred → LightGBM L1 (400 trees, 15 leaves, learning rate 0.04). Alone 0.872, yet it received 20–25% Caruana weight in every held-out fold. The three branches together: 0.816.
  • CYPMol blend, weight 0.1. The equal mean of nine recipes over the ten-member CYPMol vectors: the four isoform blocks × {TabPFN 2.6, LightGBM} plus an all-blocks TabPFN, each refit on all 1,457 labelled rows. The blend was moment-matched, so it changes ranking only (mean |Δ| 0.011, Spearman 0.9996 with the base). OOF change −0.0018 [−0.0037, +0.0002], better in 4 of 5 folds. It was the only late blend that also held up on the leaderboard.

5. Placement, and what the leaderboard told me

Every regression column is rescaled to a fixed mean and standard deviation (1A2 4.412 / 1.169, 2C9 4.830 / 0.923, 2D6 3.570 / 0.883, 3A4 4.880 / 1.082), so only its ranking comes from the model. The means come from another participant's public analysis (SuperCowPowers), who inferred moments of the blind labels by submitting affine-transformed predictions and reading the leaderboard. A second participant's reproduction kit reused those constants and I adopted them; the spreads were set against that analysis and my own leaderboard results. On 2026-09-13 this moved the Live ST-RAE for 2D6 from 1.354 to 0.566, more than any model change that followed.

No model was fitted on test labels, and I never had any. But the leaderboard acted as an information source about the test set in five places, and a reader should weigh the ranks above accordingly:

  1. The fixed means and spreads above.
  2. The classification positive rates (about 7.06% for 2D6 and 29.16% for 3A4). I estimated them from leaderboard rows of entries that predicted no positives. They fixed the call counts (49 and 249) and I steered the pseudo-negative pool to a similar rate (7.73%).
  3. The choice of which columns to send: 1A2 and 3A4 were reverted to their best-observed versions after the later versions did worse on Live.
  4. The calibration of the 2D6 robust branch and CYPMol blend, which was matched to a previously scored vector.
  5. The choice of the final 2D6 TDI score, which relied on agreement with seven earlier Live transitions.

The Final board is full-test and independent of the Live half, so it is the fairer number, but it does not remove 1 and 2: the same constants are in the final columns.

6. The classification models

CYP2D6 TDI: pseudo-negatives from the primary screen

Every supplied 2D6 TDI label is on a molecule that was a hit in the primary screen; the weakest has log2fc −0.59. The positive rate I estimated for the blind set (about 7.06%) implies that most of its scored molecules are not of that kind. A model trained on the labels alone has never seen the easy negatives. I added the 2,693 unlabelled molecules with primary log2fc ≥ −0.25 as assigned negatives and excluded the borderline band. The resulting pool has 1,497 official labels plus 2,693 pseudo-negatives, 4,190 molecules with 7.73% positives.

The rule was checked where labels exist. Among 638 labelled 1A2, 2C9 and 3A4 TDI rows at log2fc ≥ −0.25 there are zero positives (exact 95% upper bound 0.47%). Inducing the same coverage defect on 3A4 and repairing it with the device recovers the whole recoverable gap on labels no arm saw.

On Live this payload moved 2D6 MCC from 0.118 to 0.364. Reconstructed from the published bootstrap aggregates, that move was one more true positive and three fewer false positives among about 26 scored positives, which is inside an exchangeable null. On the full test set the Final value is 0.3046, against 0.2573 for the 09-24 entry.

CYP3A4 TDI: seven-member equal mean

Raising the call count to 275 on development evidence cost 0.046 MCC on Live and was reverted. Eleven later pool and gate variants did not beat it.

7. Validation, and why the Live board misled

Regression used a series-grouped five-fold split that keeps every connectivity series inside one fold, with a scaffold split as a fixed secondary readout. Classification used UMAP-cluster and scaffold five-fold. Uncertainty came from grouped molecule bootstraps. Members were combined with equal means where defensible and with Caruana or exhaustive subset selection, with step counts taken from inner folds.

The recurring failure was transfer. From late October I searched hard for better regression columns. The search included a nested selection over 728 out-of-fold columns from 406 runs and frozen embeddings from MONROE, MiniMol, OrbMol, CYPMol and fifteen CheMeleon variants. Several blends improved development ST-RAE by 0.004–0.009. Of the late blends I sent, only the 2D6 CYPMol blend held up on Live (−0.003). The rest were worse, while the unchanged 2C9 column reproduced exactly as a control. A post-mortem located the reason. The blends helped the bulk of the cross-validation cohort and hurt the rows that are analogs of training anchors, which are the closest stand-in for the official analog-expansion test set. On those rows the incumbent was the best single column by a wide margin.

If I did this again, I would make the analog rows the primary validation objective from day one. A gain below about 0.01 ST-RAE should not count as a decision.

8. What did not work

  • Fine-tuning MONROE, with challenge auxiliaries or after external CYP pretraining: worse than its own frozen readout.
  • Frozen MONROE, MiniMol, OrbMol-v2 and CYPMol embeddings in the regression ensembles: blends moved ST-RAE by at most 0.006, and none transferred.
  • Fifteen CheMeleon pretraining-target variants × six depths × two readouts (192 members) for 3A4: best 0.466 against the incumbent's 0.395. The members differ in representation but share their errors.
  • Boltz-2 trunk features for TDI: better ranking, no gain at the call boundary. A blend that used them dropped Live 2D6 MCC from 0.309 to 0.105.
  • Replacing the pseudo-negative LightGBM with a MONROE-pretrained one: Live 2D6 0.364 → 0.336, reverted.
  • Changing call counts (2D6 75/95/112, 3A4 275): indistinguishable in development, or worse on Live.
  • Further affine recalibration beyond the fixed placement: worse on Live, reverted.

9. Conclusion

Mid-table in regression, near the top in classification. Looking back, three things did real work:

  • Placement: fixing the output mean and spread, a one-line change that beat every model change.
  • Pseudo-negatives: reading the primary screen as a source of labels the TDI data was missing.
  • Leaving well alone: the final entries are the best-observed columns, after a month of searches that did not transfer.

On a personal note, this went less well than the PXR challenge, and I will be honest about why. I lost momentum around the midpoint. The last weeks were mostly a search for small blends that the leaderboard kept refusing, and I was running it out of habit more than conviction. As before, most of the work went through AI coding agents, and everything ran on one home PC. The research log, including every failed run, is public in the working repository below.

Thanks to the OpenADMET team for running the challenge and releasing the data, and to the participants who shared their code and analyses publicly. Some of the members above are built directly on that shared work.
